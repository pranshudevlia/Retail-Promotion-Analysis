# Retail Promotion Effectiveness and Discount Sensitivity

This project studies how promotions, discounts, coupons, and campaign exposure are associated with grocery purchasing behavior. It uses the **dunnhumby The Complete Journey** dataset, which contains household transactions, products, coupons, and campaign assignments.

The work is an exploratory and statistical analysis of observed data. It does not claim that a promotion caused a purchase or that a promotion generated profit.

## Questions addressed

1. How do sales and quantity differ between transactions with and without recorded discounts?
2. Which products, departments, and categories show stronger observed discount response?
3. How are coupon distribution, campaign assignment, and coupon redemption related?
4. What repeat-purchase patterns are visible after coupon redemption?
5. Which actions are supported by the evidence, and which require a randomized test?

## Data source

The source is **The Complete Journey** dataset from dunnhumby. The supplied files include `transaction_data.csv`, `product.csv`, `hh_demographic.csv`, `coupon.csv`, `coupon_redempt.csv`, `campaign_table.csv`, `campaign_desc.csv`, and `causal_data.csv`.

Download the dataset from dunnhumby's official source-file page or another legitimate provider. Place the original CSV files in `data/raw/` without changing their names or contents. The dataset may have licensing or redistribution restrictions, so raw data and generated row-level data are excluded from GitHub.

## Notebook guide

`01_data_audit.ipynb` inventories the source files and checks columns, data types, date ranges, keys, missing values, duplicates, and units. It documents data-quality decisions and creates the processed tables used later.

`02_promotion_eda.ipynb` describes the promotion mix and compares observed transaction and household outcomes across discount conditions. It defines valid denominators and uses paired household summaries where repeated observations would otherwise appear independent.

`03_discount_sensitivity.ipynb` examines product, department, category, and household-level patterns related to recorded discounts. Sensitivity is treated as an observed response measure, not formal price elasticity, because prices were not randomly assigned and important confounders are unavailable.

`04_coupon_campaign_analysis.ipynb` validates the relationships among campaigns, coupons, and redemptions. It reports assignment and redemption rates using explicit denominators, compares appropriate campaign cohorts, and describes later eligible-product purchases.

`05_business_recommendations.ipynb` summarizes effect sizes, uncertainty, limitations, and operational implications, then proposes a randomized household or store-level experiment for measuring incremental impact.

## Key findings from the current run

The following figures come from the aggregate outputs generated by the notebooks. They are descriptive or observational associations, not causal promotion effects.

- The audit covered 92,353 products, 801 demographic records, 124,548 coupon mappings, 2,318 coupon redemption events, 7,208 campaign assignments, and 30 campaign descriptions. The source tables had no missing cells in the audited fields. The coupon table contained 5,164 exact duplicate mappings, which were handled during cleaning.
- Discounted transaction lines represented about half of the analyzed lines: retail-only lines were 49.43%, while lines with no recorded discount were 49.17%. These shares describe transaction mix, not incremental sales.
- The paired household basket analysis included 2,387 households. The median discounted-versus-undiscounted basket difference was 19.90 sales-value units, with a bootstrap 95% interval of 19.32 to 20.58. The analysis did not reach its planned sample size of 3,586 households, so this result remains an observed association.
- Household discount exposure and mean quantity had a positive Spearman association of 0.176 across 783 households, with a 95% interval of 0.109 to 0.243. This is a weak association and may reflect household, product, or purchasing differences.
- Campaign redemption rates were 15.96% for TypeA, 7.91% for TypeB, and 7.67% for TypeC. These rates use assigned household-campaign rows as the denominator.
- Among the exclusive TypeA and TypeB household cohorts, the observed redemption rates were 13.39% and 10.00%. The estimated difference was 3.39 percentage points, with a 95% interval from -4.76 to 11.53 percentage points; the comparison was not statistically conclusive.
- Among redemption events, the observed 30-day eligible-product repeat-purchase rate was 36.02%. There is no untreated counterfactual in this calculation, so it should not be interpreted as retention caused by coupons.

These results support prioritizing randomized holdout experiments and adding cost, margin, inventory, and substitution measures before making economic decisions.

## Methodology and safeguards

- Raw files are preserved and inspected before transformation.
- Missing values and duplicates are measured before exclusions.
- Comparison groups and rate denominators are defined explicitly.
- Household-level pairing or aggregation is used when repeated records would violate independence assumptions.
- Effect sizes, sample sizes, confidence intervals, test statistics, and p-values are reported together where inference is appropriate.
- Statistical significance is kept separate from practical business significance.
- Promotion comparisons are observational because assignment may reflect customer behavior, product selection, seasonality, inventory, or other confounding factors.
- The dataset does not provide reliable cost or margin information, so the project makes no profit claims. Sales and quantity are treated as observed outcome proxies.

## Running the project

Create a virtual environment and install the dependencies:

```bash
python -m venv .venv
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
```

Place the licensed source CSV files in `data/raw/`, then run the notebooks in this order: `01_data_audit.ipynb`, `02_promotion_eda.ipynb`, `03_discount_sensitivity.ipynb`, `04_coupon_campaign_analysis.ipynb`, and `05_business_recommendations.ipynb`.

From the repository root, a notebook can be executed with:

```bash
jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute notebooks/01_data_audit.ipynb --inplace --ExecutePreprocessor.timeout=1200
```

Repeat the command for the remaining notebooks in the order above. Generated outputs are written to `data/processed/`.

## Limitations and next step

This is an observational retail analysis. A difference between discounted and non-discounted purchases cannot be interpreted as incremental lift without controlling for how households and products were selected for promotion. The data also does not establish profit impact, long-term customer value, or causal price elasticity.

The strongest next step is a pre-registered randomized test using eligible households or stores, an untreated holdout group, clearly defined outcomes, and margin, stockout, substitution, and customer-experience guardrails.

## Project scope

The analysis evaluates observed associations between retail promotions or discounts and grocery purchasing behavior. It covers transaction sales and quantities, product and category response, coupon distribution and redemption, campaign-associated behavior, repeat purchasing, and practical recommendations.

The project uses only files and columns that are present in the downloaded source data. Before analysis, the notebooks inspect filenames, schemas, data types, keys, date coverage, missing values, duplicates, and units. Raw files are preserved, and every important transformation or exclusion is documented.

The analysis does not assume that promotion assignment was random. Results may be affected by customer selection, product selection, seasonality, inventory, and other confounding factors. Features used to describe an outcome are kept consistent with the available timing of the data to reduce leakage.

## Analysis standards

Each notebook follows the same standards:

- Print row and column counts, missing-value counts, duplicate counts, data types, and relevant descriptive statistics before cleaning.
- Define comparison groups and denominators before calculating rates.
- Use descriptive comparisons when causal or independent observations cannot be justified.
- For inferential questions, state the objective, null and alternative hypotheses, significance level, assumptions, and power target.
- Report sample size, effect size, uncertainty, test statistic, p-value, and a plain-English conclusion where appropriate.
- Use tests that match the data structure, including paired or household-level methods when observations are repeated or clustered.
- Use clear variable names and document non-obvious analytical choices.
- Give every chart a title, labeled axes, readable values or percentages, and an appropriate scale.
- End each notebook with a summary, business interpretation, limitations, and a recommended next step.

## Analysis plan

The notebooks are intentionally focused and should be run in sequence. The data audit creates the documented analytical foundation, the next three notebooks perform the promotion, discount, and coupon analyses, and the final notebook synthesizes the evidence for a business audience. Large cleaning pipelines are not duplicated unnecessarily; later notebooks use audited processed outputs where appropriate.
